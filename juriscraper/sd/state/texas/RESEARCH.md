# Texas Courts Research

## Court Structure

Texas has a unique court structure with TWO high courts at the apex - one for civil matters and one for criminal matters. This is unique among the 50 states.

### 1. Supreme Court of Texas
- **Website**: https://www.txcourts.gov/supreme/
- Highest court for CIVIL matters only
- 9 Justices (1 Chief Justice + 8 Justices)
- Justices elected to staggered six-year terms in statewide elections
- Governor may appoint to fill vacancies (subject to Senate confirmation)
- Requirements: at least 35 years old, Texas citizen, licensed to practice law in Texas, 10+ years as practicing lawyer or judge
- Location: Supreme Court Building, 201 W. 14th Street, Room 104, Austin, Texas 78701
- Phone: (512) 463-1312
- Fax: (512) 463-1365

#### Jurisdiction
- Final appellate jurisdiction over civil cases
- Administrative control over the State Bar of Texas
- Sole authority for licensing attorneys in Texas
- Appoints members of the Board of Law Examiners
- Promulgates Texas Rules of Civil Procedure, Appellate Procedure, Evidence, and other rules

### 2. Court of Criminal Appeals
- **Website**: https://www.txcourts.gov/cca/
- Highest court for CRIMINAL matters only
- 9 Judges (1 Presiding Judge + 8 Judges)
- Judges elected to staggered six-year terms in statewide elections
- Location: Supreme Court Building, 201 W. 14th Street, Room 106, Austin, Texas 78701
- Phone: (512) 463-1551
- Fax: (512) 463-7061

#### Jurisdiction
- Final appellate jurisdiction over all criminal cases
- Death penalty cases go directly here (bypassing Courts of Appeals)

## Opinion Types and Document Categories

### Supreme Court
- **Opinions**: Full written opinions with precedential value
- **Orders**: Procedural orders, grants/denials of petition for review
- **Per Curiam Opinions**: Unsigned opinions by the court as a whole
- **Concurring Opinions**: Separate opinions agreeing with result
- **Dissenting Opinions**: Separate opinions disagreeing with result

### Court of Criminal Appeals
- **Opinions**: Published opinions on the merits
- **Orders**: Habeas corpus relief grants/denials, PDR grants/refusals
- **Per Curiam**: Unsigned opinions
- **Concurring/Dissenting Opinions**: Separate opinions
- **Statements**: Individual judge statements

### Document Categories on Handdown Lists
- Habeas Corpus Relief Granted - Opinions by the Court
- Appellant's Motion for Rehearing Denied
- State's Motion for Rehearing Denied
- Appellant's Petition for Discretionary Review Granted
- State's Petition for Discretionary Review Granted
- Appellant's Petition for Discretionary Review Refused
- State's Petition for Discretionary Review Refused
- Applications for Writ of Habeas Corpus Denied Without Written Order
- Applications for Writ of Habeas Corpus Dismissed Without Written Order
- Applications for Writ of Habeas Corpus Remanded to Trial Court
- Motion for Leave to File Original Application for Writ of Mandamus Denied
- Motion for Leave to File Original Application for Writ of Habeas Corpus Denied
- Applications for Writ of Habeas Corpus Dismissed with Written Order
- Applications for Writ of Habeas Corpus Denied with Written Order
- Abuse of Writ Order Previously Entered

## Case Number Formats

### Supreme Court
- Format: `{YY}-{NNNN}` (e.g., `24-0135`)
- YY = Last two digits of year filed
- NNNN = Sequential case number

### Court of Criminal Appeals
- **Petition for Discretionary Review (PDR)**: `PD-{NNNN}-{YY}` (e.g., `PD-0523-25`)
- **Writ of Habeas Corpus**: `WR-{NN,NNN}-{NN}` (e.g., `WR-82,126-02`)

### Courts of Appeals
- Format: `{NN}-{YY}-{NNNNN}-{CR/CV}` (e.g., `01-23-00531-CR`)
- NN = Court number (01-15)
- YY = Year
- NNNNN = Case number
- CR = Criminal, CV = Civil

## Technical Implementation Notes

### Website Architecture
- **Primary Domain**: www.txcourts.gov (main court portal)
- **Search System**: search.txcourts.gov (TAMES - Texas Appellate Management and eFiling System)
- **Case Mail**: casemail.txcourts.gov (notification system)
- All courts share the same search infrastructure

### Opinion File Format
- All opinions provided as PDF documents
- URLs use GUID/UUID for MediaVersionID and MediaID
- PDFs hosted at both:
  - search.txcourts.gov/SearchMedia.aspx (dynamic)
  - www.txcourts.gov/media/{id}/{filename}.pdf (static)

### Data Refresh
- Data refreshed nightly (not real-time)
- Hand down lists available after 9:00 AM on release date

### Search System Features
- Case Search: By case number, style, attorney, date filed, county, etc.
- Document Search: Search within case documents
- Supports filtering by court (all courts or specific courts)
- Can filter by case type (Civil/Criminal/Both)

### Data Availability
- **Court of Criminal Appeals Hand Downs**: 1958 to present (some gaps)
- **Supreme Court Orders & Opinions online**: October 2014 to present
- **Historical data**: Available via separate archives

## Notes

1. **Two High Courts**: Texas is unique in having separate high courts for civil (Supreme Court) and criminal (Court of Criminal Appeals) matters.

2. **15 Intermediate Appellate Courts**: Texas has the most intermediate appellate courts of any state. The 15th Court of Appeals was created in 2023 with exclusive statewide jurisdiction over certain civil cases involving state agencies.

3. **Overlapping Jurisdiction**: The 1st and 14th Courts of Appeals serve the same 10 counties in the Houston area; cases are randomly assigned between them.

4. **Death Penalty**: Death penalty cases bypass the Courts of Appeals and go directly to the Court of Criminal Appeals.

5. **Electronic Filing**: Mandatory e-filing through eFile Texas for most courts.

6. **TAMES System**: The Texas Appellate Management and eFiling System (TAMES) provides centralized case search and document access for all appellate courts.

7. **Hand Down Schedule**:
   - Supreme Court: Typically Fridays
   - Court of Criminal Appeals: Typically Wednesdays
   - Courts of Appeals: Varies by court (often Tuesdays and Thursdays)

8. **Petition for Discretionary Review (PDR)**: The mechanism for appealing from a Court of Appeals to the Supreme Court (civil) or Court of Criminal Appeals (criminal).

9. **Rules Changes**: The Supreme Court's rules of practice changed on January 1, 2026, affecting petitions for review filed on or after that date.

10. **Business Courts**: Texas has Business Court divisions in several major cities (Dallas, Houston, San Antonio, Austin) for complex commercial litigation.
