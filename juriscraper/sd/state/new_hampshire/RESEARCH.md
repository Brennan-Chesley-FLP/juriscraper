# New Hampshire Courts Research

## Court Structure

New Hampshire has a simple appellate court structure with only one appellate court - the Supreme Court. There is no intermediate Court of Appeals.

### 1. New Hampshire Supreme Court
- **Website**: https://www.courts.nh.gov/our-courts/supreme-court
- Highest and only appellate court in New Hampshire
- 5 Justices (1 Chief Justice + 4 Associate Justices)
- Justices nominated by the Governor, confirmed by Executive Council
- Lifetime terms or until age 70
- Located at One Charles Doe Drive, Concord, NH (Frank Rowe Kenison Supreme Court Building)

#### Jurisdiction
- Appellate jurisdiction over State trial courts (Circuit and Superior Courts)
- Appellate jurisdiction over State administrative agencies
- Jurisdiction to issue writs of certiorari, prohibition, and habeas corpus
- Constitutional interpretation
- Administration of the courts (Chief Justice is administrative head)

### Trial Courts (for reference)
- **Superior Court**: General jurisdiction trial court (11 locations)
- **Circuit Court**: Limited jurisdiction court with three divisions:
  - District Division
  - Family Division
  - Probate Division

## Case Number Format

New Hampshire Supreme Court case numbers follow the pattern: `YYYY-NNNN`

### Examples
- `2024-0465` - Donald McGowan & a. v. Town of Hampton
- `2025-0133` - In the Matter of Tara Dickison and Thomas Sileo
- `2024-0407` - State of New Hampshire v. Christopher C. Crosby
- `2025-0056` - Peregrine Interests LLC v. Todd

### Case Types (from case titles)
- Criminal (State of New Hampshire v. [Defendant])
- Civil (Party v. Party)
- Family (In the Matter of...)
- Administrative appeals (Appeal of...)
- Guardianship (In re Guardianship of...)

## Opinion Types and Publication

### Neutral Citation Format (Adopted January 1, 2024)
- Format: `YYYY N.H. NN`
- Example: `2025 N.H. 54` (54th opinion of 2025)
- Notice: https://www.courts.nh.gov/sites/g/files/ehbemt471/files/inline-documents/sonh/1-18-2024-notice-concerning-adoption-of-neutral-citation.pdf

### Prior Citation Format (2023 and earlier)
- Listed by Supreme Court case number
- Cited to New Hampshire Reports once published

### Opinion Categories
1. **Opinions**: Published decisions with precedential value
2. **Supervisory Orders**: Administrative orders not related to specific cases
3. **3JX Final Orders**: Three-justice panel final orders
4. **Case Orders**: Procedural orders in specific cases

### Publication Process
- Opinions subject to motions for rehearing under Rule 22
- Subject to formal revision before publication in New Hampshire Reports
- Errors reported to: reporter@courts.state.nh.us

### Document Formats
- All opinions and orders: PDF format
- Oral argument recordings: Video (Vimeo)

## Technical Implementation Notes

### Website Structure
- **Primary Domain**: courts.nh.gov
- **Supreme Court Section**: /our-courts/supreme-court
- **Asset Storage**: /sites/g/files/ehbemt471/files/
  - Documents: `/documents/{YYYY-MM}/`
  - Inline documents: `/inline-documents/sonh/`

### Opinions Page Structure
- Dynamic loading with JavaScript
- Filterable by keyword
- Sortable: Date (New-Old), Alphabetical (A-Z), Reverse Alphabetical (Z-A)
- Paginated results (5, 10, 15, or 25 per page)
- Each entry shows:
  - Citation (e.g., "2025 N.H. 54")
  - Case title
  - Case number in parentheses
  - Document format (PDF)
  - Date
  - Related documents (if applicable)

### Oral Arguments Page Structure
- HTML table format
- Columns: Date Argued, Case Number, Case (with parties/attorneys), Video Link
- Video links to Vimeo

### Scraping Approach
1. **Opinions**: Parse yearly opinion pages for PDF links
   - Note: Dynamic JavaScript loading may require browser automation
2. **Supervisory Orders**: Parse yearly supervisory order pages
3. **Oral Arguments**: Parse yearly recording pages for Vimeo links
4. **RSS/Feeds**: No obvious RSS feed available for opinions

### Date Handling
- Opinion dates displayed as: MM/DD/YYYY
- PDF paths use: YYYY-MM format

## Notes

1. **No Intermediate Appellate Court**: New Hampshire is one of the few states without an intermediate Court of Appeals. All appeals go directly to the Supreme Court.

2. **Unified Court System**: Established in 1971, making the Chief Justice responsible for efficient operation of all courts.

3. **Constitutional Courts**: Both Supreme Court and Superior Court became constitutional courts in 1966 (can only be changed by constitutional amendment).

4. **Neutral Citation**: Adopted January 1, 2024, following the pattern `YYYY N.H. NN`.

5. **Dynamic Website**: Opinion pages use JavaScript for filtering and pagination, which may require browser automation for scraping.

6. **Video on Vimeo**: Oral argument recordings are hosted externally on Vimeo rather than on the court's own servers.

7. **E-Filing Available**: Electronic filing available through the court's electronic services portal.

8. **Privacy Policy**: https://www.nh.gov/policies/privacy-policy

9. **ADA Compliance**: https://www.courts.nh.gov/resources/americans-disabilities-act-ada
