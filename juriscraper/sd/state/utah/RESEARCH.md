# Utah Courts Research

## Court Structure

Utah has a unified court system with two appellate courts at the apex.

### 1. Utah Supreme Court
- **Website**: https://www.utcourts.gov/en/about/courts/appellate-courts/sup.html
- Highest court in Utah (court of last resort)
- 5 Justices serving 10-year renewable terms
- Chief Justice elected by majority vote for 4-year term
- Associate Chief Justice serves 2-year term
- Location: Scott M. Matheson Courthouse, 450 South State Street, Salt Lake City, Utah 84114

#### Jurisdiction
- Original jurisdiction for certified questions from federal courts and extraordinary writs
- Appellate jurisdiction over:
  - First degree and capital felony convictions
  - Civil judgments (non-domestic)
  - Public Service Commission proceedings
  - Tax Commission proceedings
  - School and Institutional Trust Lands Board of Trustees
  - Board of Oil, Gas, and Mining
  - State Engineer
  - Certiorari review of Court of Appeals decisions
  - Judicial Conduct Commission proceedings
  - Constitutional and election questions

#### Schedule
- Law and motion calendar: First and third Mondays of each month
- Oral arguments: First week of every month
- Opinion release: Thursdays at 10:00 a.m.

### 2. Utah Court of Appeals
- **Website**: https://www.utcourts.gov/en/about/courts/appellate-courts/coa.html
- Created in 1987
- 7 Judges serving 6-year renewable terms
- Presiding Judge elected by majority vote for 2-year term
- Sits in rotating panels of 3 judges (prohibited from sitting en banc by statute)
- Primary location: Salt Lake City (travels to other regions several times per year)

#### Jurisdiction
- Appeals from Juvenile and District Courts (except small claims)
- Domestic relations cases: divorce, annulment, property division, child custody, support, visitation, adoption, paternity
- Criminal matters less than first degree or capital felony
- Administrative agency appeals: Utah Industrial Commission, Department of Employment Security, Career Service Review Board
- Cases transferred from Supreme Court

#### Schedule
- Oral arguments: Third and fourth weeks of each month
- Law and motion panel: One day per month
- Opinion release: Thursdays at 10:00 a.m.

### 3. Trial Courts
- **District Courts**: General jurisdiction (8 judicial districts)
- **Juvenile Courts**: Juvenile delinquency, child welfare, termination of parental rights
- **Justice Courts**: Limited jurisdiction (Class B/C misdemeanors, infractions, traffic, small claims)

## Opinion Types and Document Categories

### Supreme Court
- **Published Opinions**: Full written opinions with precedential value
- **Per Curiam Opinions**: Unsigned opinions
- **Concurring Opinions**: Separate opinions agreeing with result
- **Dissenting Opinions**: Separate opinions disagreeing with result

### Court of Appeals
- **Published Opinions**: Precedential opinions
- **Unpublished Decisions** (1997-2010): Non-precedential (archived separately)
- **Juvenile Case Decisions**: Available from 2005 to present
- **Memorandum Decisions**: Brief decisions (prior to 2010 rule change)

### Business and Chancery Court
- **Orders**: Available from 2025 to present

## Notes

1. **Unified Court System**: Utah operates a unified state court system, meaning all courts operate under the administrative direction of the Supreme Court.

2. **Retention Elections**: Utah uses a merit selection and retention election system for appellate judges. Judges are initially appointed by the Governor and then face periodic retention elections.

3. **Court of Appeals Panels**: The Court of Appeals is statutorily prohibited from sitting en banc (all judges together). Cases are decided by rotating 3-judge panels.

4. **Legacy Site**: Much of the substantive content (opinions, calendars, audio) remains on the legacy.utcourts.gov subdomain, while the main site (www.utcourts.gov) has been modernized.

5. **Appellate Mediation**: The Court of Appeals has an Appellate Mediation Office (established 1998) for alternative dispute resolution.

6. **Judicial Districts**: Utah has 8 judicial districts for trial-level courts.

7. **CORIS System**: The Courts Information System (CORIS) is used for case management. Most district courts have used it since ~1998.

8. **Pro Bono Program**: The Appellate Courts' Pro Bono Pilot Program assists self-represented parties in appeals.

9. **eFiling**: Appellate court eFiling is available at https://www.utcourts.gov/en/legal-help/legal-help/procedures/filing/efiling/appellate.html

10. **Petitions for Certiorari**: Listed separately at https://www.utcourts.gov/en/about/courts/appellate-courts/sup/certs.html
