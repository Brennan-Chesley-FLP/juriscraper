# South Dakota Courts Research

## Court Structure

South Dakota has a Unified Judicial System administered by the Chief Justice, consisting of a single appellate court (the Supreme Court) and circuit courts. South Dakota does NOT have an intermediate Court of Appeals.

### 1. South Dakota Supreme Court
- **Website**: https://ujs.sd.gov/supreme-court/
- Highest and ONLY appellate court in South Dakota
- 5 Justices (1 Chief Justice + 4 Associate Justices)
- Justices are appointed by the Governor from nominees selected by the Judicial Qualifications Commission
- Must be licensed to practice law in South Dakota
- Must be voting residents of the district from which they are appointed
- Face nonpolitical retention election 3 years after appointment, then every 8 years
- Chief Justice elected by and from among the five justices for a 4-year term
- Location: 500 E. Capitol Ave., Pierre, SD 57501
- Phone: (605) 773-4885
- Clerk Email: SCClerkBriefs@ujs.state.sd.us

#### Jurisdiction
- Appellate jurisdiction over circuit court decisions
- Original jurisdiction in cases involving interests of the state
- Issues original and remedial writs
- Renders advisory opinions to the Governor on issues involving executive power
- Rule-making authority over court practice and procedure
- Administrative leadership for the legal and judicial system
- Controls admissions to and disciplines members of the State Bar

### 2. Circuit Courts (Trial Courts)
- **Website**: https://ujs.sd.gov/circuit-courts/
- General jurisdiction trial courts
- 7 judicial circuits covering 66 counties
- Handle civil, criminal, family, and juvenile matters

## Opinion URL Patterns

### Published Opinions (PDFs)
```
https://ujs.sd.gov/media/{hash}/{case_number}.pdf
```
Where:
- `{hash}` = Random hash string (e.g., `wb0plwaw`, `hx1bh21d`)
- `{case_number}` = Case number (e.g., `31017`, `30969`)

## Opinion Naming Convention

South Dakota Supreme Court opinions follow the citation format:
```
{CASE_NAME}, {YEAR} S.D. {NUMBER}
```

Examples:
- `ESTATE OF WEBB, 2026 S.D. 2`
- `ESTATE OF O'NEILL, 2026 S.D. 1`
- `LJP CONSULTING, LLC v. VERVENT, INC., 2025 S.D. 74`
- `STATE v. BITELER, 2025 S.D. 73`

## Opinion Types and Document Categories

### Published Opinions
- Full written opinions with precedential value
- Citation format: `{YEAR} S.D. {NUMBER}`
- Available online from 2006 to present
- 1,879 total opinions in database (as of January 2026)

### Summary Dispositions
- Cases decided without full written opinion
- Includes disposition type (e.g., "Affirmed") and judge name
- Available from 2022 to present
- 329 total summary dispositions in database

### Term of Court Calendar
- Cases scheduled for oral argument
- Distinguishes between "Oral" and "Non Oral" cases
- Includes case briefs and calendar documents
- Available from 2002 to present
- 4,168 total entries

## Notes

1. **No Intermediate Appellate Court**: South Dakota is one of only 10 states without an intermediate Court of Appeals. All appeals from circuit courts go directly to the Supreme Court.

2. **Term of Court Schedule**: The Supreme Court holds Terms of Court multiple times per year in Pierre. The February 2026 Term is scheduled for Feb. 10-12, 2026.

3. **Unified Judicial System**: All courts operate under the administrative supervision of the Chief Justice.

4. **Nonpartisan Retention Elections**: Justices face retention elections rather than contested elections.

5. **Judicial Districts**: The 5 Supreme Court districts are different from the 7 circuit court judicial circuits.

6. **PDF-Based Documents**: All opinions, orders, and briefs are provided as PDF downloads.

7. **eCourts System**: Requires free registration for case search; documents cost $0.10/page up to $3.00 max per document.

8. **Live Streaming**: Supreme Court oral arguments can be streamed live during Term of Court via the homepage.

9. **YouTube Archive**: Past oral arguments and State of the Judiciary addresses are available on the SDUJS YouTube channel.

10. **Summary Disposition Judges**: The judge listed in summary dispositions appears to be the circuit court judge whose decision was affirmed, not a Supreme Court justice.
